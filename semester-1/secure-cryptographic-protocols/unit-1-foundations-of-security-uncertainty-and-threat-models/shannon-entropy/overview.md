# 📖 Overview

## 📊 Shannon Entropy

Entropy is one of the most fundamental concepts in **Information Theory**, **Cryptography**, and **Cybersecurity**. Introduced by **Claude E. Shannon** in 1948, Shannon Entropy provides a mathematical framework for measuring the **uncertainty**, **randomness**, or **information content** of a random event.

In cryptography, security is closely related to unpredictability. The more unpredictable a password, cryptographic key, or secret message is, the more difficult it becomes for an attacker to guess or infer it. Shannon Entropy quantifies this unpredictability using probability theory, allowing us to measure how much information is produced by an event and how uncertain an attacker remains about a secret.

Unlike traditional security concepts that focus on preventing unauthorized access, Shannon Entropy focuses on **measuring uncertainty mathematically**. This makes it one of the most important tools in information-theoretic security and the foundation for concepts such as **Conditional Entropy**, **Mutual Information**, and **Perfect Secrecy**.

***

### 🎯 Why Study Shannon Entropy?

Understanding Shannon Entropy is essential because it enables us to:

* Measure the uncertainty of random events.
* Quantify the amount of information contained in data.
* Evaluate the unpredictability of passwords and cryptographic keys.
* Analyze information leakage in secure communication.
* Understand the mathematical foundations of modern cryptography.
* Build a strong foundation for advanced information-theoretic concepts.
