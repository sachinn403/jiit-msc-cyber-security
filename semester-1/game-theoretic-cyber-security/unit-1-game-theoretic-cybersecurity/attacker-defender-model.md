# Attacker–Defender Model

##

### Overview

The **Attacker–Defender Model** is a game-theoretic representation of cybersecurity, where two players—the **attacker** and the **defender**—make strategic decisions to achieve opposing objectives. The final outcome depends on the actions taken by both participants. :contentReference\[oaicite:1]{index=1}

***

### Players

#### Attacker

* Attempts to compromise the system.
* Chooses an attack strategy (e.g., malware, phishing, exploitation).

#### Defender

* Protects the system from attacks.
* Chooses an appropriate defense strategy (e.g., patching, firewall, IDS).

***

### How It Works

1. The **attacker** selects an attack strategy.
2. The **defender** selects a defense strategy.
3. Both strategies interact.
4. The final outcome depends on the decisions made by both players. :contentReference\[oaicite:2]{index=2}

***

### Example

**Attacker**

* Chooses **Malware**

⬇️

**Defender**

* Chooses **Intrusion Detection System (IDS)**

⬇️

**Outcome**

* Depends on the decisions of both the attacker and the defender. :contentReference\[oaicite:3]{index=3}

***

### Key Takeaways

* The Attacker–Defender Model is a fundamental concept in Game Theory for Cyber Security.
* Both players act strategically to maximize their own objectives.
* Neither player can determine the final outcome independently.
* The interaction between both strategies determines the result.
