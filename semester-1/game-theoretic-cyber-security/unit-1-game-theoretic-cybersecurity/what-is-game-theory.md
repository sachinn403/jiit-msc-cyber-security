# What is Game Theory?

### Definition

**Game Theory** is a branch of **mathematics** that studies strategic interactions among **rational decision-makers**, where the outcome for one participant depends on the decisions made by others. It provides a mathematical framework for analyzing situations involving **competition** and **strategic decision-making**. :contentReference\[oaicite:0]{index=0}

In simple terms, Game Theory helps us understand how individuals or organizations make decisions when the success of one depends on the actions of others.

***

### Key Characteristics

* A branch of **mathematics** that focuses on strategic decision-making.
* Studies interactions between **two or more rational players**.
* Assumes that every player aims to **maximize their payoff**.
* The outcome depends on the **decisions of all players** involved.
* Applicable to situations involving **competition, cooperation, and conflict**. :contentReference\[oaicite:1]{index=1}

***

### Also Known As

Game Theory is also referred to as:

* Theory of Games
* Competitive Strategy Theory
* Strategic Decision Theory :contentReference\[oaicite:2]{index=2}

***

### Questions Addressed by Game Theory

Game Theory helps answer questions such as:

* What strategy should a player choose?
* How will competitors react?
* Which decision will produce the best possible outcome? :contentReference\[oaicite:3]{index=3}

***

### Cyber Security Example

Consider the following scenario:

* A **hacker** decides whether to attack a server.
* A **system administrator** decides whether to apply a security patch.
* The success or failure of the attack depends on the **decisions of both players**.
* Game Theory helps analyze the strategies of both the attacker and the defender to determine the most effective course of action. :contentReference\[oaicite:4]{index=4}

***

### Key Takeaways

* Game Theory is the mathematical study of **strategic decision-making**.
* It analyzes situations where one player's outcome depends on the decisions of others.
* It provides a systematic framework for studying **competition, cooperation, and conflict**.
* In cybersecurity, it helps model **attacker–defender interactions** and supports effective security decision-making.

***
