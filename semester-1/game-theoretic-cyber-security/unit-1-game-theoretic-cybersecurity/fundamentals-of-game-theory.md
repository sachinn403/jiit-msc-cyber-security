# Fundamentals of Game Theory

### Introduction

The fundamentals of **Game Theory** describe the basic principles that govern strategic interactions between players. These principles explain how rational decision-makers choose strategies while considering the possible actions of others to achieve the best possible outcome. :contentReference\[oaicite:0]{index=0}

***

### Fundamental Characteristics

#### 1. Rational Players

* Every player acts **rationally**.
* Each player aims to **maximize their own payoff or benefit**.
* Decisions are made to achieve the best possible outcome. :contentReference\[oaicite:1]{index=1}

#### 2. Strategic Decisions

* Every player's decision depends on the **expected actions of other players**.
* Players carefully evaluate different strategies before making a decision.
* The selected strategy aims to maximize the expected reward. :contentReference\[oaicite:2]{index=2}

#### 3. Interdependence

* The success of one player depends on the **decisions made by other players**.
* No player makes decisions in complete isolation.
* Every action can influence the overall outcome of the game. :contentReference\[oaicite:3]{index=3}

#### 4. Conflicting Objectives

* Players usually have **opposing goals**.
* One player's gain often becomes another player's loss.
* This creates strategic competition between participants. :contentReference\[oaicite:4]{index=4}

***

### Cyber Security Example

Consider the following scenario:

* A **hacker** attempts to compromise a system.
* A **defender** deploys security mechanisms to prevent the attack.
* Both players choose strategies based on the expected actions of the other.
* The outcome depends on the decisions made by **both participants**. :contentReference\[oaicite:5]{index=5}

***

### Key Takeaways

* Game Theory is based on **rational decision-making**.
* Players choose strategies while considering the actions of other players.
* The decisions of all players are **interdependent**.
* Strategic interactions often involve **conflicting objectives** between participants.
